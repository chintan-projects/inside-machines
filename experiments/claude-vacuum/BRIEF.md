## Hypothesis and teaching contract

After watching and changing one physically connected air path, a beginner can
explain that a powered fan moves air, air carries loose dirt, and a porous bag
catches dirt while air continues through it. They can predict that an ideally
airtight blocked inlet prevents sustained pickup despite a rotating fan.

Grant: make the pressure/flow relationship visible with the object, then reconnect
the whole. Feynman: introduce each job in plain words; name it after the job.
Veritasium: invite an optional prediction before the blocked-path reveal. Anatomy:
use the actual kit for well-made filled solids, four-tone volume, connected parts,
real fittings, sensible painter order, a fan with a shaft/motor and a real nozzle.
Project overrides: core labels anchored on scene, opt-in motion, semantic air/dirt
encodings. No endorsement or imitation of a creator's personal style is implied.

Tenets: the object earns attention before copy; follow one parcel/path rather than
many competing arrows; teach three causal jobs rather than a parts inventory;
one meaningful experiment earns its controls. Optional depth follows satisfaction.
Choose coherent tokens with a reason (ink, material, type, space, motion); the
production UI's palette/layout is not a constraint. Default is still and legible.

## Storyboard and assessment

Claude chooses the composition and beat count; no prior layout is provided. Teach
question/prediction, observation, fan→air, air→loose dirt, bag→separation, then whole
system and transfer. The attention sequence must say these jobs operate together.
For every beat identify a labeled focus, input, action/reason, output and visible
evidence. A colored shell alone cannot explain an internal mechanism. Closeups
must show their own working part and surrounding passage/walls, with a return to
the whole. Static steps must preserve the explanation when motion is off.

Teach-back: why does turning the fan help pick up loose dirt? Score 2: fan moves air,
surrounding air enters and carries dirt; 1: air/suction named without mechanism;
0: motor attracts dirt or another incompatible explanation.
Transfer before reveal: the motor sounds normal, but the hose is kinked airtight.
Will it keep collecting loose dirt? Score 2: no sustained throughflow, so no air
transport despite rotation; 1: correct direction without why; 0: unchanged pickup.
Prediction/answer/skip optional, answer only revealed deliberately, no text storage.
Initial target: ≥4/5 initially unfamiliar beginners score 2 for both, not yet tested.
