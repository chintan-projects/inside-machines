## Storyboard / authoritative rules

Core: recognizable cutaway → heat enters the colder internal coil → liquid
evaporates → compressor takes vapor and adds work → hot outside coil rejects
heat/condenses → narrow tube drops pressure and returns cold mixture → whole
loop reconnects. These operations run together while the compressor operates;
attention sequence is not an on/off sequence. Refrigerant stays in sealed tubing,
never mixes with food air. Insulation slows heat entry; no electrical repair.

Large evaporator inset includes tube wall, surrounding cabinet air and a working
liquid/vapor passage. Compressor inset shows a supported reciprocating example
with cylinder/piston, inlet and outlet; a generic fridge may use another design.
The domain provides compression position/phase and all qualitative outcomes.
No drawing-only physics, invented thermometers, pressure gauges or exact cycle time.

Insight: 3 illustrative units removed +1 unit of work →4 released. Door closed
separates cabinet/room. Door open gives the same room 4−3=1 net. Units are an
energy-accounting example, not appliance efficiency, rate or temperature.
Phase model: compare the same fluid at a chosen cold temperature equal to the
lower-pressure boiling point and below the higher-pressure one. At lower pressure it can boil; at higher pressure it stays
liquid in this chosen comparison. No dynamic pressure-change simulation; sensible
warming is possible even without boiling. Do not imply all heat transfer stops.

Grant: stable coil/heat identities and explicit 3+1=4 transformation.
Feynman: heat enters a colder fluid; pressure makes boiling possible at a cold
temperature, before naming evaporator/condenser. Veritasium: door-open prediction
and reveal; transfer to a system whose hot side is actually outside the room.
Anatomy: substantial cabinet, fittings, coils and compressor; own-context insets.

## Transfer / acceptance

Core: if the outside coil cannot pass heat to its surroundings, why is cooling
impaired? Score 2: heat must be rejected for sustained operation; not merely
because a component name is missing. Do not suggest trying this physically.
Insight: if the hot coil is outside the kitchen, could the cold side then remove
heat from the kitchen? Score 2: yes in principle, because rejected heat/work are
sent outside its boundary. Phase transfer: if a tube region contains only vapor,
can it still absorb heat by evaporating liquid there? Score 2: no liquid remains
to evaporate, but the vapor can still take in heat and warm.
