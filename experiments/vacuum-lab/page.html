A little more physics

Electricity produces magnetic forces in the motor, turning its shaft. The shaft turns the fan. The blades transfer energy to air.

Air pressure is the push from surrounding air. The fan maintains a pressure difference, so air flows through the cleaner. Surrounding air pushes toward the lower-pressure space.

A fan sitting on a table also transfers energy to air. The cleaner adds a directed path and a way to separate dirt.

The bag is porous: air can cross its material. It doesn’t trap every particle; real cleaners may use additional filters.

Does the idea travel?

The bag is clear, but the hose is kinked completely shut. The motor is running. What happens to fresh dirt at the nozzle—and why?

Think it through, then reveal

Fresh dirt stops entering in this idealized example. The kink breaks the air path. A spinning fan alone cannot keep carrying dirt through a sealed passage.

About this experiment, sources & authoring briefs

What we’re comparing

One OpenAI Codex interactive coding session authored four local prototypes using the actual named libraries. Narration, claims, chapter timing and the blocked-inlet condition are held constant. The visual direction and its implementation both vary.

This is a first comparison of authored approaches. It does not rank API models, measure autonomous generation reliability, or prove one engine is intrinsically better. No paid app-model calls were made. Speech is the same local computer voice for every version.

Where the simplified picture stops

A generic bagged cleaner—not a particular appliance. Air and dirt markers are symbols, not measurements. The blocked inlet is ideally airtight; leaks, bypass paths and protection controls are omitted. Use the on-screen experiment only.

Still to validate

Independent technical review, an owner walkthrough on a real phone, and beginner teach-back/transfer. Our target is curious people roughly 8–80; that age range has not yet been validated.
