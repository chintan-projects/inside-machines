# Fixed teaching brief used in this OpenAI/Codex authoring session

Make a visually compelling, truthful explanation for a curious person aged 8–80.
Question: what actually picks up dirt in a generic bagged vacuum cleaner?
Target: one useful causal mental model in about 45 seconds, with optional extra depth.
Use the exact shared story.json claims, chapter timing and narration across candidates.

Build the argument: powered fan moves air out and lowers intake-side pressure;
surrounding room air moves through the nozzle/hose and carries loose dirt;
a porous bag retains some dirt while air crosses the bag material and continues
to the fan and outlet. These are simultaneous jobs, not a four-step operating cycle.

Before solving geometry, identify what visible change proves each relationship.
Make the recognizable object carry the story where useful. Introduce technical
names after their job. Invite one prediction; do not require a quiz to watch.
Demonstrate an ideally airtight blocked inlet with a continuing fan but no sustained
incoming air/dirt. State the qualitative scope; do not invent flow rates, pressure,
RPM, universal fan geometry or complete filtration. Keep debris upstream of the fan.

Read ADR 0001, the CE spec and the claim ledger. Use one common pure state to drive
motion and conditions; the adapter owns presentation, not another set of physics.
Render actual output from the named library. A renamed lookalike SVG is not a valid
comparison. Keep labels readable on a phone, preserve component identity, avoid
decorative particle storms, and show the complete connected path at the end.

Inspect the real rendered frames at phone size. Fix the highest-impact error,
not another palette variation. Do not present model critique as human acceptance.
